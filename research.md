## Publications

- **De Kleer, Dirck**, Catherine E. De Vries, and Simon van Teutem. "Public Support for Pro-Environment and Environment-Critical Protests." <span style="color: #0000EE; font-weight: bold;">British Journal of Political Science</span>.

  <details class="abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>With environmental protests on the rise, we ask: how do they affect support for pro-environment and environment-critical movements? We answer this question using evidence from two studies—a survey experiment and media content analysis—conducted in the Netherlands, a leading country in the green transition. Our experimental findings reveal an asymmetric bias in public support for protests. For the same protest action, public support is higher for environment-critical movements compared to pro-environment ones. This bias is most pronounced among right-leaning individuals with low education and low trust in science and politics. Our content analysis traces the bias back to newspaper reporting. While attention to protest groups is balanced across tabloid and broadsheet newspapers, tabloid reporting is more negative about pro-environment movements. These results highlight an important aspect of the backlash against environmental policies: a bias against pro-environment movements within parts of the public and media.</p>
  </details>

## Working Papers

- **De Kleer, Dirck**. "The Far Right and Loss of Parliamentary Decorum: Evidence from Committee Debates." (draft upon request)

  <details class="abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>Parliamentary politics relies on decorum to structure political conflict. Yet we know little about when this adherence breaks down. I examine whether the presence of far-right MPs leads to a loss of parliamentary decorum. I argue that far-right MPs deliberately break decorum to cultivate an outsider image, and that this provokes mainstream MPs to respond in kind. I test this argument with a novel dataset of nearly 800,000 speeches from Dutch parliamentary committee debates (2008–2026). I use a parsimonious measurement strategy, measuring losses in decorum as instances where MPs fail to speak through the chair. Leveraging variation in far-right attendance across meetings, I find that the presence of far-right MPs leads to decorum losses. These are concentrated among both far-right and mainstream MPs. The results suggest that the democratic consequences of far-right politics extends to the conduct of legislative work, where their mere presence erodes the norms that keep parliamentary conflict impersonal.</p>
  </details>

- **De Kleer, Dirck**, and Alonso Román Amarales. "Does Far-Right Success Increase Hate Crimes? Evidence from U.S. House Elections (2006–2022)." (draft upon request)

  <details class="abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
  <p>Far-right actors increasingly win elections across democracies, yet little is known about the behavioral consequences of their electoral victories. We argue that their electoral victories increase the visibility and perceived legitimacy of exclusionary views. This lowers the social costs of acting on pre-existing prejudice, leading to increased hostility toward minority groups even in the absence of local policy change. We test this argument using a sharp regression discontinuity design around close U.S. House elections between 2006 and 2022. We find that the electoral victory of far-right Republican candidates increases reported hate crimes targeting LGBTQ+ persons in their districts. Additional analyses of media coverage, campaign communications, and survey data show that victors receive sustained public attention and prominently emphasize anti-LGBTQ+ positions. We do not find evidence of changes in mass attitudes toward minorities. Our findings demonstrate that electoral victories, by themselves, can have important behavioural consequences, independent of policy implementation.</p>
  </details>
